    What Is Network Monitoring, and Does a Small Business Need It?

    Shannon Jones
    Jan 19, 2024
    3 min read

    Updated: Sep 28

    Updated September 27, 2026 · By Shannon Jones, Roo Tech Services


    The short answer: Network monitoring is software that watches your internet connection, firewall, switches, Wi-Fi and computers around the clock and alerts your IT provider when something looks wrong. For a small business, it means problems like a failing device, a full disk or suspicious traffic get caught and fixed before they turn into downtime or a breach.


    What does network monitoring actually watch?

    A monitoring system checks in on the equipment that keeps your business connected, usually every few minutes:


    • Your internet connection: Is it up? Is it slow? Is the backup connection working?

    • Your firewall: Is it online, up to date and blocking threats?

    • Switches and Wi-Fi access points: Are they responding? Is one overloaded?

    • Servers and computers: Are disks filling up? Is a service stopped? Did a backup fail?

    • Security events: Unusual traffic, repeated failed logins, or connections to known malicious sites.


    When something crosses a threshold, it creates an alert. The important part is what happens next: a person needs to look at it and act.


    Why does it matter for a small business?

    Without monitoring, you usually find out about IT problems the same way: something stops working in the middle of a busy day. With monitoring, many of those problems are caught early.


    • A hard drive showing early signs of failure gets replaced before it dies.

    • A firewall that went offline after a power blip gets noticed in minutes, not the next morning.

    • A backup that silently failed for a week gets fixed before you need it.

    • Suspicious traffic from an infected computer gets flagged before it spreads.


    What are the common obstacles?

    Monitoring sounds simple, but a few things trip businesses up:


    Too many alerts. A poorly tuned system sends so many alerts that people stop reading them. Good monitoring is tuned so the alerts that come through matter.


    Nobody watching. Monitoring software that emails a shared inbox nobody checks doesn't help. Someone has to be responsible for responding, including after hours.


    Blind spots. Devices added later, like a new printer, camera system or Wi-Fi access point, often never get added to monitoring.


    Old equipment. Consumer-grade routers and unmanaged switches often can't report their health at all.


    Monitoring without security. Watching whether devices are up is only half the job. You also want a firewall that inspects traffic for threats and reports what it blocks.


    What does good network monitoring look like?

    • Every important device is included, and new ones get added as they're installed.

    • Alerts go to a person who acts on them, with clear response times.

    • The firewall is managed and its security subscriptions are current.

    • You get simple reports on what happened and what was fixed.

    • Monitoring is paired with patching, backups and endpoint protection, so problems get fixed, not just noticed.


    Frequently asked questions

    Do small businesses need 24/7 network monitoring?

    If your business depends on its internet, phones, email or a server, yes. Problems don't wait for business hours, and catching an issue overnight often means it's fixed before your team arrives.


    Is network monitoring the same as a firewall?

    No. A firewall controls and inspects the traffic coming in and out of your network. Monitoring watches the health and activity of all your equipment, including the firewall, and alerts someone when something's wrong. You want both.


    Can network monitoring stop a cyberattack?

    Monitoring alone doesn't block attacks, but it can spot the warning signs early, like unusual traffic or a device talking to a known malicious site, so someone can respond quickly. Paired with a managed firewall and endpoint protection, it's a key part of a layered defense.
